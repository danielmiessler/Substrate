# PR-00003 — The Adult AI Capability Gap

**Category:** Problem
**Scale:** National (United States)
**Created:** 2026-07-24
**Referenced by:** [PL-00002](../Plans/PL-00002—US_Buildout_Plan.md)
**Solved by:** [SO-00003](../Solutions/SO-00003—Universal_Free_AI_And_Business_Training.md)

---

## Statement

The United States has 170.7 million people in its civilian labor force
([BLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_force_in_the_United_States)) and a federal workforce
training system that reaches roughly 238,000 of them a year. That is 0.14%. The gap is not a funding
shortfall against a working system; it is a system built for a different problem.

## Evidence

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US civilian labor force | 170.7 million (record, Jan 2025) | [BLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_force_in_the_United_States) |
| WIOA Title I annual appropriation | ~$2.9B (FY2026, roughly level with FY2025) | [CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R44252) |
| Title I split | $885.7M adults, $948.1M youth, $1.09B dislocated workers | [CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R44252) |
| People trained through WIOA (PY2020) | 237,836 | [TCF](https://tcf.org/content/report/beyond-job-placement-reimagining-wioa-for-economic-mobility-and-workforce-resilience/) |
| Cost per WIOA participant | $1,500–2,500 | [TCF](https://tcf.org/content/report/beyond-job-placement-reimagining-wioa-for-economic-mobility-and-workforce-resilience/) |
| Cost per participant, sectoral programs that work | $8,000+ | [TCF](https://tcf.org/content/report/beyond-job-placement-reimagining-wioa-for-economic-mobility-and-workforce-resilience/) |
| Share of WIOA adult participants receiving training | <50% | [TCF](https://tcf.org/content/report/beyond-job-placement-reimagining-wioa-for-economic-mobility-and-workforce-resilience/) |

## The two failures

**Reach.** 238,000 a year against 170.7 million is a rounding error. Even a tenfold expansion of the
existing system reaches 1.4% of the labor force.

**Depth.** The programs that produce durable wage gains — sectoral programs with wraparound support
— cost more than three times what WIOA spends per participant. The system is simultaneously too
small and, per participant, too cheap to work.

## Compounding factor

The buildout in [PL-00002](../Plans/PL-00002—US_Buildout_Plan.md) needs roughly 100,000 new
electricians and line workers a year against a Department of Labor projection of 81,000 openings a
year purely for replacement ([CEWD](https://cewd.org/resources/energy-workforce-fast-facts/)). Those
people do not currently exist in the labor force. No amount of energy funding creates them.

## Falsifiers

- Federal training programs reach more than 5 million people in a year.
- Per-participant spend in federal training rises above $6,000 without a reach collapse.
- Independent evaluation shows AI-fluency training producing no measurable income effect.
