## Statement

The United States does not manufacture the autonomous machines it depends on, and it under-deploys
the ones it has. Both halves are measurable.

## Evidence — the import half

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DJI share of US drone market | ~77% | [ElectroIQ](https://electroiq.com/stats/dji-statistics/) |
| DJI share of global civilian drone market | ~70% (2025), down from 74% | [The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2025/11/06/2025-drone-market-dji/) |
| China share of world commercial drone production | ~90% | [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/reality-check-breaking-free-from-chinas-drone-ecosystem-is-harder-than-you-think/) |
| China share of global drone exports (2024) | >70% | [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/reality-check-breaking-free-from-chinas-drone-ecosystem-is-harder-than-you-think/) |
| Time to build sufficient US LFP battery capacity for military drone demand | ≥5 years | [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/reality-check-breaking-free-from-chinas-drone-ecosystem-is-harder-than-you-think/) |
| DoD Drone Dominance target | 150,000 units by 2028 | [DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/05/13/drone-dominance-us-drone-supply-chain-xponential-2026/) |

The dependency is at the component layer: airframes, batteries, motors, cameras, radios, displays.
A US-assembled drone with Chinese cells, motors, and radios is an import with extra steps. The
December 2025 ban on new foreign-manufactured UAS removes the supply without replacing it.

## Evidence — the deployment half

| Country / region | Robots per 10,000 employees | Source |
|---|---|---|
| South Korea | 1,220 | [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas) |
| Western Europe | 267 | [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas) |
| **United States** | **307 (8th globally)** | [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas) |
| North America | 204 | [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas) |
| China | 166 (revised methodology, World Robotics 2025) | [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas) |

US industrial robot installations were **38,000 units in 2025**, up 11%, with automotive at 13,500
([IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/us-robot-industry-returns-to-double-digit-growth)).
Against 12.6 million manufacturing employees
([BLS, April 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)), 307 per 10,000 implies an
installed base near **387,000 robots** (derived). Korea's density on the same employment base would
be **1.54 million**.

## Why it matters to the rest of the plan

Electricity is the input; autonomy is what converts it into output. A country that quadruples its
generation and imports its machines has bought the world's most expensive way to run someone else's
industrial base.

## Falsifiers

- US robot density passes 600 per 10,000 before 2032.
- Domestic drone component production (cells, motors, radios) serves >50% of US demand before 2030.
- Annual US robot installations exceed 90,000 before 2030.
