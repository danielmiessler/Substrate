# CL-00001 — 10× US Energy Capacity in Four Years Is Physically Impossible

**Category:** Claim
**Created:** 2026-07-24
**Status:** Supported
**Confidence:** High
**Referenced by:** [PL-00002](../Plans/PL-00002—US_Buildout_Plan.md)

---

## Claim

The United States cannot multiply its electric generating capacity by ten within four years, and
the obstacle is physical rather than financial.

## Argument style

Deductive. If the required build rate exceeds the world's entire demonstrated build rate by a large
multiple, and the end state exceeds the world's entire installed base, the target cannot be met
regardless of funding.

## Argument

1. US installed generating capacity is approximately **1,300 GW** as of end-2025. This is derived:
   EIA reports **1,189,492 MW** of utility-scale net summer capacity at end-2023 plus **47,704 MW**
   of small-scale solar ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/electricity-in-the-us-generation-capacity-and-sales.php)),
   and **53 GW** was added in 2025 alone ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67205)).

2. A 10× target implies an end state of **13,000 GW** and net additions of **11,700 GW** over four
   years, a required rate of **2,925 GW/yr**.

3. The largest year of US capacity additions on record is **53 GW (2025)**, the most since 2002. The
   largest planned year is **86 GW (2026)** ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67205)).
   The required rate is therefore **34× the record year**.

4. The entire world added **692 GW** of renewable capacity in 2025, its largest increase ever
   ([IRENA via Earth.org](https://earth.org/renewables-hit-nearly-half-of-global-power-capacity-in-2025/)).
   The required rate is roughly **4× the whole planet's annual buildout**, sustained for four years,
   by one country.

5. Total world installed capacity across all sources is approximately **9,400 GW** (2023)
   ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/267358/world-installed-power-capacity/)). The
   4-year end state of 13,000 GW would exceed **all generating capacity ever built by human
   civilization, by 38%**.

6. Four independent supply constraints each bind well below the required rate:
   domestic solar cell capacity of **3 GW/yr**
   ([SEIA](https://seia.org/news/united-states-surpasses-50-gw-of-solar-module-manufacturing-capacity/));
   large transformer lead times of **128 weeks**, up to four years for high-capacity units, with a
   modeled 30% national shortfall
   ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/));
   interconnection queues holding **2,061 GW** with a median request-to-operation time **over four
   years** ([LBNL](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/queued-2025-edition-characteristics)); and an
   electrician shortfall of **81,000/yr** before any buildout
   ([CEWD](https://cewd.org/resources/energy-workforce-fast-facts/)).

7. Money does not relieve any of the four. 2,061 GW of already-financed projects are waiting on
   process and hardware.

8. **Therefore the 10×/4-year target cannot be met, and the 16×/10-year target — requiring
   1,950 GW/yr, 23× the record — cannot be met either.**

## Corollary worth noting

The 4-year target requires a **higher annual rate** (2,925 GW/yr) than the 10-year target
(1,950 GW/yr). If one of the two is to be retained, the 10-year target is the survivable one and
the 4-year milestone should be dropped rather than the 10-year goal weakened.

## What would falsify this claim

- A single year of US capacity additions exceeding 500 GW.
- Total world installed capacity exceeding 20,000 GW before 2030, which would change the
  plausibility frame in step 5.
- A generation technology reaching commercial deployment at a per-GW build time an order of
  magnitude below current utility-scale solar.
