## Quick Context

Each row is one operation against real targets in which attackers used AI, or in which credible evidence says they probably did, from January 2023. A grade says how strong the evidence of AI use is; it does not say how bad the attack was. The count rises when more is published and found, which is not the same as more happening, and what vendors and model providers can see on their own platforms is over-represented.

---

## Methodology Summary

**Approach:** Sweep a registry of sources, disposition every candidate (admitted, merged, or excluded with a reason), check each supporting quote against the fetched text of its own sources, and grade each incident twice, the second time by a grader who did not extract it.

**Sources:** 240 swept; 270 in the registry, in 8 families: 160 vendor-ti, 53 press, 16 government, 16 incident-database, 12 model-provider, 8 regional-cert, 3 academic, 2 court-record. See [sources.csv](./sources.csv).

**Definition Used:** An incident is one operation, with real targets, where the source says AI did part of the attacker's work. Attribution is the source's, never ours. Full rules: [research/ai-hacking-incidents-2026-11/METHODOLOGY.md](../../research/ai-hacking-incidents-2026-11/METHODOLOGY.md).

## Source Analysis

### Why These Sources?

| Source family | Strengths | Weaknesses | Weight Given | Registered | Incident citations |
|---------------|-----------|------------|--------------|------------|--------------------|
| **vendor-ti** | Telemetry and forensic detail; investigates the intrusion itself | Has a commercial reason to publish; sees only its own customers | High | 160 | 151 |
| **press** | Reaches fraud, arrests and victims that vendors never see | Repeats claims; counts as primary only when it quotes a named party on the record | Medium | 53 | 83 |
| **government** | Advisories and indictments are on-record and attributable | Slow, and often silent on how AI was used | High | 16 | 12 |
| **incident-database** | Finds cases the other families miss; links to originals | Restates other reports; not counted as independent corroboration | Low | 16 | 65 |
| **model-provider** | Sees its own platform; names the model and the account | Reports only what it chooses to publish, and only on its own platform | High | 12 | 82 |
| **regional-cert** | Local-language reporting from the affected region | Terse, and hard to cross-check | Medium | 8 | 5 |
| **academic** | Systematic, with method sections | Often measures capability, not a real operation | Medium | 3 | 0 |
| **court-record** | Sworn or adjudicated; names the facts that were proven | Rare, and late | High | 2 | 1 |

Incident citations count an incident once per registered source it cites, so an incident that cites two sources of one family counts twice there.

### Key Source Conflicts

1. **Two graders disagreed** on 81 of 286 regraded incidents (28%); each disagreement has a written reason in [regrade.json](./regrade.json), and the ledger carries the final grade.
2. **One report, many operations.** A single vendor report often covers several actors. Each named actor the report describes on its own is one incident; the rest of the report is not counted.
3. **Same actor, different vendors.** 10 pairs that look alike (same named actor within a month, or the same quote) are kept as separate incidents, each with the reason in [candidates.json](./candidates.json) under `curation.distinct`.

## Alternative Estimates & Why We Differ

| Estimate | Source | What It Actually Measures | Why It Differs |
|----------|--------|--------------------------|----------------|
| **33 ledger rows** | [Vulnerability-And-Breach-Data/ai-incidents.json](../Vulnerability-And-Breach-Data/ai-incidents.json) | One row per actor or tool per primary document, defender demonstrations included | A different unit and a narrower sweep; it feeds vulnerabilitydata.io and is not replaced by this dataset |
| **14 harnesses with attacker use** | [Offensive-AI-Harnesses](../Offensive-AI-Harnesses/SUMMARY.md) | Open-source agent harnesses named in attack reports | Counts tools, not incidents; one incident can name several harnesses and most incidents name none |
| **286 incidents** | This dataset | Operations with a source, graded by strength of evidence of AI use | Counts published operations only, with attackers using AI as the test |

### Why Our Approach

- One incident is one operation with real targets, so a count means something the reader can check against the source.
- The grade is separate from severity, so a small incident with strong evidence is not ranked below a large one with weak evidence.
- Related cases we do not count (agents that left an evaluation, AI used only as infrastructure, influence operations with no hacking, researcher proofs of concept) are named in the methodology, not silently dropped.

**Key insight:** Trackers that report different numbers are usually counting different things, and none of them counts attacks.
