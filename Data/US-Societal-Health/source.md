## Series

### Alcohol Consumption per Capita (`alcoholPerCapita`)

- **Publisher:** NIAAA Surveillance Report #122, Table 1
- **Where:** <https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/sites/default/files/surveillance-report122.Per-Capita-Consumption.pdf> · <https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/surveillance-reports>
- **Unit:** gallons of pure ethanol per person aged 14+, per year
- **Coverage:** 1850–2023
- **Method:** pdftotext -layout, Table 1 rows, 'All beverages' column
- **Note:** Apparent consumption: beverage sales and tax data converted to gallons of pure ethanol (beer + wine + spirits) divided by the population aged 14 and older (15 and older before 1970). NIAAA's Alcohol Epidemiologic Data System; a new surveillance report each spring with a ~16-month lag.

### Consumer Bankruptcies (`bankruptcies`)

- **Publisher:** Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Household Debt and Credit Report (Consumer Credit Panel/Equifax)
- **Where:** <https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc>
- **Unit:** thousands of consumers with a new bankruptcy notation per year
- **Coverage:** 2003–2025
- **Method:** HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx sheet "Page 17 Data" column bankruptcy, annual sum of quarters (full years only)
- **Note:** Number of consumers with a new bankruptcy notation on their credit file, summed across the four quarters of each year, from the New York Fed Consumer Credit Panel. The spike and collapse around October 2005 is the BAPCPA bankruptcy-reform filing rush. Only full years are included. US Courts' Table F-2 filing counts are the administrative alternative; this panel series is used because it is one machine-readable file back to 2003.

### Better Off Than a Year Ago (`betterOffThanYearAgo`)

- **Publisher:** University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers, Table 6
- **Where:** <https://data.sca.isr.umich.edu/tables.php> · <https://data.sca.isr.umich.edu/data-archive/mine.php>
- **Unit:** percent saying they are better off financially than a year ago
- **Coverage:** 1978–2025
- **Method:** POST mine.php table=6 qorm=Y, column "Better"
- **Note:** "Would you say that you (and your family living there) are better off or worse off financially than you were a year ago?" Annual figures as published by the Survey Research Center. Same web-transition caveat as the expectations series.

### Better Off Than Parents (`betterThanParents`)

- **Publisher:** NORC General Social Survey (cumulative file 1972–2024)
- **Where:** <https://gss.norc.org/get-the-data/stata.html> · <https://gss.norc.org/content/dam/gss/get-the-data/documents/stata/GSS_stata.zip>
- **Unit:** percent whose standard of living is better than their parents' at the same age
- **Coverage:** 1994–2024
- **Method:** variable PARSOL: 1/2 of valid codes 1/2/3/4/5, weight WTSSPS
- **Note:** Compared to your parents when they were the age you are now, do you think your own standard of living now is much better, somewhat better, about the same, somewhat worse, or much worse than theirs was? NORC General Social Survey cumulative file, computed here from the public microdata: weighted (WTSSPS, NORC's post-stratification weight available for every year 1972–2024) share of valid responses. 2021 moved to a push-to-web design during the pandemic and 2022/2024 are mixed-mode; NORC advises caution comparing 2021+ with the in-person years, so treat 2021 as a seam. Years with fewer than 200 weighted responses are dropped.

### Credit Card Delinquency Rate (`creditCardDelinquency`)

- **Publisher:** Federal Reserve Board via FRED (DRCCLACBS)
- **Where:** <https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRCCLACBS>
- **Unit:** percent of credit card balances 30+ days past due, all commercial banks, annual average
- **Coverage:** 1991–2026 (2026 partial)
- **Method:** fredgraph.csv DRCCLACBS, annual mean of quarterly values
- **Note:** Delinquency rate on credit card loans at all commercial banks (Fed Board Charge-Off and Delinquency Rates release): loans 30 days or more past due plus nonaccrual, seasonally adjusted, as a percent of balances. Covers bank-held cards only; the New York Fed's serious (90+ day) delinquency series from the Consumer Credit Panel is in this dataset as creditCardSeriousDelinquency.

### Very Happy (`veryHappy`)

- **Publisher:** NORC General Social Survey (cumulative file 1972–2024)
- **Where:** <https://gss.norc.org/get-the-data/stata.html> · <https://gss.norc.org/content/dam/gss/get-the-data/documents/stata/GSS_stata.zip>
- **Unit:** percent of adults "very happy"
- **Coverage:** 1972–2024
- **Method:** variable HAPPY: 1 of valid codes 1/2/3, weight WTSSPS
- **Note:** Taken all together, how would you say things are these days — would you say that you are very happy, pretty happy, or not too happy? NORC General Social Survey cumulative file, computed here from the public microdata: weighted (WTSSPS, NORC's post-stratification weight available for every year 1972–2024) share of valid responses. 2021 moved to a push-to-web design during the pandemic and 2022/2024 are mixed-mode; NORC advises caution comparing 2021+ with the in-person years, so treat 2021 as a seam. Years with fewer than 200 weighted responses are dropped.
