# SO-00003 — Universal Free AI and Business Training

**Category:** Solution
**Created:** 2026-07-24
**Solves:** [PR-00003](../Problems/PR-00003—Adult_AI_Capability_Gap.md)
**Funded by:** [FS-00001](../Funding-Sources/FS-00001—AI_Infrastructure_Levy.md)
**Part of:** [PL-00002](../Plans/PL-00002—US_Buildout_Plan.md)

---

## Approach

Three tiers, free at point of use, sized to reach 50 million adults in four years. The existing
federal system reaches 237,836 a year at $1,500–2,500 per participant, while the programs that
actually move wages cost $8,000+
([TCF](https://tcf.org/content/report/beyond-job-placement-reimagining-wioa-for-economic-mobility-and-workforce-resilience/)).
The design below is deliberately barbelled: very cheap at the top of the funnel, and at genuine
sectoral cost where the outcome is a job or a business.

| Tier | Reach | Content | Per learner | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Open** | 40M adults | AI fluency: tool use, prompting, verification, judging output | $150 | $6.0B |
| **T2 — Cohort** | 8M | AI at work, live cohorts, assessment, portable credential | $800 | $6.4B |
| **T3 — Sectoral** | 2M | Independent business formation; electrician, line worker, robotics technician | $8,000 | $16.0B |
| **Total** | **50M** | | | **$28.4B over 4 years = $7.1B/yr** |

**Context: this is 1.5% of the energy program in [SO-00001](./SO-00001—Federal_Energy_Buildrate_Program.md) and 2.4× the entire current WIOA Title I appropriation of ~$2.9B**
([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R44252)).

## Why the tiers are priced the way they are

**T1 at $150** is content, platform, and assessment with no wraparound support. It is cheap because
it is not trying to be a job program. It is trying to make AI tool use as ordinary as spreadsheet
use.

**T2 at $800** buys live cohorts and a credential. This is roughly one third of what WIOA currently
spends per participant, at 33× the reach.

**T3 at $8,000** is priced at the sectoral-program level on purpose. The $1,500–2,500 figure is what
the current system spends, and the current system does not work. Underfunding this tier to raise
its reach is the specific failure mode this design exists to avoid.

## The two outputs that feed the rest of the plan

**Trades pipeline.** T3 delivers the 100,000 electricians and line workers a year that
[SO-00001](./SO-00001—Federal_Energy_Buildrate_Program.md) U4 requires. DOL projects 81,000
electrician openings a year for a decade as replacement demand alone
([CEWD](https://cewd.org/resources/energy-workforce-fast-facts/)), so the buildout's workforce does
not exist yet and cannot be hired, only made.

**Independent income.** The business-formation track is the part of this plan that is not about
building things. If [SO-00002](./SO-00002—Domestic_Autonomy_Manufacturing_Push.md) succeeds, a
meaningful number of existing jobs stop existing. A country that automates without teaching people
to build their own income has done half a plan. See
[RI-00003](../Risks/RI-00003—Automation_Displaces_The_Workers_It_Trains.md).

## Falsifiers

- T1 completion rate below 20%, which would mean reach is nominal.
- T3 placement rate below 50% into trades or sustained self-employment at 24 months.
- Electrician pipeline below 60,000/yr by 2030, which breaks SO-00001's U4.
- Per-learner cost in T3 falling below $5,000, which would indicate it has drifted back into the
  failure mode it was designed against.
