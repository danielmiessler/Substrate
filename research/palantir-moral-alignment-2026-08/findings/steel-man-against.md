# The Steel Man AGAINST Palantir

The strongest honest case a critic can make, built only from claims that survived verification. This case deliberately disowns the anti-Palantir folklore (data-selling, Lavender attribution, the Muslim registry)—see `fact-check.md`—because the documented record is stronger without it.

## 1. The company's stated ethical constraints failed the only real test they ever faced

In October 2020, Palantir wrote to Amnesty International that it had "purposefully declined to take on contracts with ERO and CBP under the current Administration because we share your organization's concern with the potential serious human rights violations against migrants, refugees, and asylum seekers." That is a written, published claim of moral agency: we can decline, and we did.

In April 2025, under an administration whose enforcement posture was harsher—meaning the concern Palantir itself cited had grown, not shrunk—the company signed the ERO-requested ImmigrationOS contract, whose own procurement language covers "streamlining selection and apprehension operations," "self-deportation tracking... near real-time visibility," and the "end to end immigration lifecycle from identification to removal." Its current position is that contractors "should not be in a position to set policy."

Note what this sequence is: the same company, the same divisional line it once drew, opposite behavior, and a rewritten justification that retroactively denies the moral agency it previously claimed and exercised. This is the cleanest documented case in the record where the company's ethics and its commercial-political incentives pointed in opposite directions. The incentives won, and the principles were restated to match. A stated ethic that binds only when it costs nothing is indistinguishable from marketing.

## 2. The candor record is impeachable on exactly the claims that can be checked

- "We do not work for E.R.O."—contradicted by FOIA: DHS's own documents state ERO personnel used ICM, and the 2017 unaccompanied-children initiative was explicitly joint ERO/HSI (443 arrests, 35 criminal).
- "Palantir does not and has not supported predictive policing workflows... anywhere" (May 2025, in a letter to the UN)—a sentence readers can weigh against the New Orleans program (predicting who would commit or suffer violence), the LAPD's chronic-offender scoring, and a Palantir crime-forecasting patent.
- The bin Laden legend—never asserted by the company, never corrected by it, and its own website hosted the column that launched it.

The inference is the argument: this is a company asking for trust on classified, unauditable deployments, and on the small subset of its claims that CAN be publicly verified, it has repeatedly shaded the truth. Visible candor is the only available predictor of invisible candor.

## 3. The documented harms are audits and court records, not vibes

- 443 relatives and caregivers of migrant children arrested through an ICM-mediated initiative; 35 criminal arrests among them.
- 680 people arrested in the 2019 Mississippi raids, with Palantir-processed infrastructure in the workflow, on the first day of the school year.
- The LAPD Inspector General's audit: roughly half the "chronic offenders" on the Palantir-powered list had at most one violent-crime arrest; about a tenth had no quality police contact at all. (The list was 84% Black and Latino; the audit noted this mirrored arrest demographics—the deeper problem it documents is a feedback loop where surveillance produces the contacts that justify more surveillance, per Sarah Brayne's peer-reviewed LAPD ethnography.)
- HessenDATA ran thousands of times a year, including against people with no connection to any crime, on a legal basis Germany's Constitutional Court voided in 2023.
- Medicaid data, collected for healthcare administration, reached ICE's ELITE targeting application in 2026; asked what was done to purge it, officials pointed to a file deleted from a Microsoft Teams chat.

## 4. Gaza: the confirmed version, which needs no myth

Set aside the unsupported Lavender attribution entirely. What is confirmed: Palantir announced its own strategic partnership with Israel's Ministry of Defense in January 2024, during a war then under active ICJ genocide proceedings, held its board meeting in Tel Aviv around signing it, and has never disclosed what its software does there. A UN Special Rapporteur found "reasonable grounds to believe" its platform supports military targeting (an allegation threshold, from a contested rapporteur—but a formal one that triggers a duty of disclosure the company has not met; its response attacked the report's framing and answered nothing operational). Separately, Amnesty documented in August 2025 that Palantir tooling poses surveillance risks to pro-Palestine student protesters and migrants inside the United States. The pattern in one frame: maximum-stakes deployment, self-announced, zero disclosure, and dissent at home surveilled by the same vendor's stack.

## 5. Where opacity is optional, Palantir chooses more of it

Classification is imposed by the customer and binds any contractor; that portion of the opacity is not Palantir's fault. But a consistent pattern of the opacity is chosen:

- New Orleans was structured as a philanthropic donation, which meant no procurement process and no city council review; key officials did not know the program existed for six years.
- The NHS contract shipped with data-protection sections redacted; NHS England later apologized after admitting Palantir engineers could access identifiable patient data in ways its own impact assessment had not reflected.
- The privacy advisory council is compensated, confidential, and advisory, by Palantir's own description.
- When a Swiss magazine reported unflattering facts about its failed government bids, Palantir sued (a right-of-reply action, the mildest available instrument, but litigation against a newsroom nonetheless); the court rejected 22 of 23 requests and assessed 95% of costs to Palantir.

## 6. The governance makes the unaccountability permanent

Class F shares dynamically top the three founders up to 49.999999% of any vote regardless of how much stock they sell; the 2026 annual-meeting arithmetic confirms the insider block was almost exactly half of votes cast. Under that structure, shareholder proposals for an independent due-diligence report and a human-rights impact assessment failed (Norway's sovereign wealth fund voted for both). No independent third-party audit of any Palantir deployment exists in the public record. The accountability vacuum is not an oversight; it is an annually ratified design, and it means every external check that has ever worked on this company (journalism, FOIA, courts, customer-side auditors) operated from outside and against resistance.

## 7. The worldview of the people with permanent control

The men holding locked-in control of the West's data-integration layer state their views openly: the chairman wrote "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible"; the CEO's manifesto declared some cultures "dysfunctional and regressive" and called for remilitarizing Germany and Japan; the company celebrates its role in the kill chain. These are stated positions, not inferences. A reader can decide how much weight they carry; what they cannot support is the claim that this is a values-neutral instrument of whatever the electorate decides.

## What the critic must concede

To remain a steel man: the data-selling, Lavender, and Muslim-registry claims are false or unsupported and should be retired; the audit-logging architecture is a real improvement over the legacy baseline, acknowledged in government privacy assessments; the Ukraine work is real, verified, and morally weighty; the China/Russia refusal is real; named individual victims traceable to specific software outputs are largely absent from the public record, and the untested counterfactual (what agencies would do with worse tools) is a genuine hole in the harm case.
