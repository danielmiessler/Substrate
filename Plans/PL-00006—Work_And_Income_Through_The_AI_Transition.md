# PL-00006 — Work and Income Through the AI Transition

**Author:** Daniel Miessler · **Created:** 2026-07-24 · **Status:** Draft v1 · **Type:** Substrate Plan
**Part of:** [The US Program](./README.md) · **Pairs with:** [SO-00003](../Solutions/SO-00003—Universal_Free_AI_And_Business_Training.md)

> **The one-liner:** The jobs are going. The work isn't. The plan is to move 170 million people from holding jobs to owning capability.

---

# SCOPE

What happens to American work and income as AI absorbs task after task. This is the plan the rest of
the program exists to make survivable, and the one most likely to be wrong, because the underlying
rate of change is not yet measurable with confidence.

---

# WHERE WE ARE

| Signal | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Net US jobs displaced by AI, monthly | **~16,000** (25,000 eliminated, 9,000 created) ≈ 192,000/yr | [Goldman Sachs, Apr 2026](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-job-displacement-statistics/) |
| Entry-level job postings, YoY | **−15%** | [Axis](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-job-displacement-statistics/) |
| Software developer employment, ages 22–25 | **−20% since 2024** | [Axis](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-job-displacement-statistics/) |
| Employment, ages 22–25 in AI-exposed roles | **−13%** | [Axis](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-job-displacement-statistics/) |
| Occupations impacted by AI by 2030, advanced economies | **60%** | [Axis](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-job-displacement-statistics/) |
| Projected labor force change by 2032 | **−5.9 million (−3.7%)**, driven by aging | [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://www.hiringlab.org/2026/05/14/how-a-shrinking-workforce-ai-and-labor-reallocation-will-define-the-next-15-years/) |
| Civilian labor force | 170.7 million | [BLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_force_in_the_United_States) |

**Read those rows together and the picture is specific rather than apocalyptic.** 192,000 net
displacements a year is small against a 170-million labor force — about 0.1%. But it is not spread
evenly: it is concentrated almost entirely on people entering the workforce, in exactly the roles
that used to be how a career started. The ladder is losing its bottom rungs while the building is
still standing.

Meanwhile demographics remove 5.9 million workers by 2032 for reasons that have nothing to do with
AI. **The country is going to have both a labor shortage and a displacement crisis at the same time,
in different places.** Any plan that treats this as one problem gets it wrong.

---

# CHALLENGES

1. **The entry-level rung is disappearing.** −15% postings and −20% for young software developers
   is not a forecast, it is a measurement. You cannot have senior people in ten years without
   junior people now.
2. **Displacement and shortage are simultaneous.** Construction, grid, trades and care work are
   short hundreds of thousands of people while clerical and junior knowledge work contracts.
3. **Identity is attached to jobs, not to capability.** When the job goes, meaning goes with it,
   which is the part no income policy addresses.
4. **The rate is unknown.** 60% of occupations "impacted" spans everything from a better spreadsheet
   to an eliminated role. Anyone claiming precision here is selling something.

---

# MISSION

1. Make **capability**, not employment, the unit of economic security.
2. Rebuild the entry rung so a 22-year-old has a path that does not depend on a job that no longer
   exists.
3. Move as many people as possible from working *for* an organization to producing value they own
   — the Human 3.0 transition.

---

# STRATEGIES

### S1 — Fund the person, not the job

Training, credentialing, tooling and startup capital attach to the individual and travel with them.
**Target: every adult has a funded, portable capability account by 2030.** This is
[SO-00003](../Solutions/SO-00003—Universal_Free_AI_And_Business_Training.md) delivered as an
entitlement rather than a program: 50 million adults, $7.1B/yr, less than 2% of the
[buildout budget](./PL-00002—US_Buildout_Plan.md).

### S2 — Rebuild the entry rung deliberately

If firms no longer hire juniors because AI does junior work, the training that used to happen
inside firms has to happen somewhere. **Target: 1 million funded apprenticeships a year by 2032**,
across trades, care, grid, and knowledge work. Pay the apprentice, subsidize the supervision, and
require a real output.

### S3 — Point the displaced at the shortage

The country is short 81,000 electricians a year
([PL-00002](./PL-00002—US_Buildout_Plan.md)), needs 250,000 construction trades
([PL-00003](./PL-00003—Housing_Abundance.md)), and is automating claims processing
([PL-00004](./PL-00004—Healthcare_That_Costs_What_Its_Worth.md)). **Target: displaced-to-shortage
placement within 12 months for anyone who wants it.** These are the same people, and no mechanism
currently connects them.

### S4 — Make owning a business the default option

Most people have never been shown how. **Target: 5 million new independent businesses by 2036**,
via the business-formation track in
[SO-00003](../Solutions/SO-00003—Universal_Free_AI_And_Business_Training.md) T3 — formation, pricing,
distribution, and the AI leverage that makes one person able to run what used to take ten.

### S5 — Measure it honestly, monthly

**Target: a public displacement dashboard** — net AI displacement by occupation, entry-level
postings, time-to-placement, business formation rate — published monthly on a fixed schema. The
policy response has to be able to change when the rate changes, and it cannot if the rate is a press
release.

---

# WHAT CHANGES

1. Losing a job is a transition of months, not a fall off a cliff.
2. A 22-year-old has an entry path that is funded and real.
3. Millions of people own the thing they do instead of renting themselves to someone who owns it.
4. The country stops arguing about whether AI takes jobs and starts reading a number every month.

# HOW THIS FAILS

- **The rate could be far faster than 192,000/yr.** Every number above is a 2026 measurement of a
  process that is not linear. This plan's sizing is the part most likely to be wrong.
- **Business formation is not for everyone**, and a program that assumes it is will fail the people
  who most need it.
- **Apprenticeships need firms to host them**, and the firms automating junior work are the firms
  being asked to supervise juniors.
- **Retraining programs have a bad track record** — see
  [PR-00003](../Problems/PR-00003—Adult_AI_Capability_Gap.md). The response is to fund at sectoral
  levels rather than at the $1,500-per-participant level that produced the track record.

---

# WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THIS PLAN

- Entry-level postings recovering on their own by 2028, which would mean the rung is cyclical.
- Net AI displacement exceeding 1 million/yr, which would mean this plan is sized an order of
  magnitude too small and needs replacing rather than adjusting.
- Capability accounts showing no income effect at 24 months against a matched control.
