## Mechanism

A **15% levy on hyperscaler capital expenditure** on AI and data center infrastructure.

## The justification

Data centers consumed **176 TWh, 4.4% of US electricity, in 2023** and are projected at **325–580
TWh, 6.7–12%, by 2028**
([LBNL/DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-in-electricity-demand-from-data-centers)).
The firms creating that load are the same firms spending $725B/yr on the infrastructure creating it.
This is not a windfall tax on profits. It is a charge on the capital deployment that generates the
demand, levied at the moment the capital exists.

## Why capex and not profit

Profit is portable and can be reported anywhere; capex is physical and lands where the concrete is
poured. It is also the metric these firms guide publicly and quarterly, which makes the base
observable rather than negotiated. See
[FS-00003](./FS-00003—Profit_Shifting_Recovery.md) for why the profit base is the harder one.

## Risks

- Levying capex disincentivizes the domestic buildout it is meant to fund. A US-siting credit
  against the levy is the obvious mitigation and is not modeled here.
- Capital is mobile. See [RI-00002](../Risks/RI-00002—AI_Levy_Capital_Flight.md).
- The base is volatile: it grew 77% in one year and could contract as fast.

## Falsifiers

- Combined Big Four capex falling below $400B/yr, which halves the yield.
- Static scoring of a 15% capex levy coming in below $80B/yr.
- Material offshoring of datacenter construction in response.
