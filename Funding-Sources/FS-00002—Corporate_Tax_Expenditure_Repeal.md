# FS-00002 — Corporate Tax Expenditure Repeal

**Category:** Funding Source
**Created:** 2026-07-24
**Addresses:** [PR-00004](../Problems/PR-00004—Corporate_Tax_Base_Erosion.md)
**Funds:** [SO-00001](../Solutions/SO-00001—Federal_Energy_Buildrate_Program.md), [SO-00002](../Solutions/SO-00002—Domestic_Autonomy_Manufacturing_Push.md)

## Mechanism

Repeal corporate tax expenditures and route the recovered revenue to the buildout.

## Yield

**$264B/yr** — total corporate tax expenditures in 2025, per JCT
([PGPF](https://www.pgpf.org/article/the-six-largest-corporate-tax-expenditures/)).

For scale, federal corporate income tax receipts were **$452.1B in FY2025**
([CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61953)). Corporate tax expenditures equal roughly **60%** of
corporate tax revenue actually collected.

### The six largest

| Provision | Annual |
|---|---|
| Credit for and expensing of research activities | $74B |
| Reduced tax rate on active income of controlled foreign corporations | $48B |
| Depreciation of equipment in excess of ADS | $34B |
| Energy credit | $19B |
| Foreign-derived intangible deduction eligible income (FDDEI) | $15B |
| Credit for low-income housing | $13B |
| **Subtotal** | **$203B — 77% of the total** |

Source: [PGPF](https://www.pgpf.org/article/the-six-largest-corporate-tax-expenditures/) citing JCT.
Total tax expenditures across individuals and corporations are projected at **$2.3T in FY2026**
([CRFB](https://www.crfb.org/blogs/jct-projects-tax-expenditures-will-be-23t-2026)); about 12% of
that goes to corporations.

## Three things that must be said honestly

**1. This is a tax increase, not a cleanup.** $264B on top of $452B collected is a ~58% increase in
corporate taxation. Calling it "closing loopholes" is a framing choice, and the plan should not
hide behind it.

**2. JCT says the base is smaller than the rhetoric.** Eliminating *every* corporate tax expenditure
only supports lowering the corporate rate to **28%**
([JCT via MLPA](https://www.mlpassociation.org/joint-committee-on-taxation-report-eliminating-all-corporate-tax-expenditures-only-lowers-rate-to-28/)).

**3. Three of the six largest fund this plan's own goals.** The R&D credit ($74B), accelerated
depreciation ($34B), and the energy credit ($19B) are exactly the provisions subsidizing domestic
solar manufacturing, robotics capex, and generation. The energy credit is a direct subsidy to
[SO-00001](../Solutions/SO-00001—Federal_Energy_Buildrate_Program.md). Repealing $127B of
buildout-aligned incentives to fund a $419B/yr buildout is partly self-cancelling.

**Recommended scope:** repeal the $137B that is not buildout-aligned (CFC rate, FDDEI, low-income
housing, and the long tail), retain or reform the R&D, depreciation, and energy provisions, and
close the difference from [FS-00001](./FS-00001—AI_Infrastructure_Levy.md) and
[FS-00003](./FS-00003—Profit_Shifting_Recovery.md). The plan's headline $264B is the *maximum*, and
the honest working figure is lower.

## Falsifiers

- JCT scoring total corporate tax expenditures below $150B in a future year.
- Dynamic scoring showing behavioral response erasing more than 40% of static yield.
- Repeal of the energy credit measurably slowing the SO-00001 rate ladder.
